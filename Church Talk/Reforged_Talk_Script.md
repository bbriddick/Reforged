# Why I Made Reforged
### An 8-minute talk — speaking script with timing cues

*Target length: ~1,050 words at a natural, unhurried speaking pace (~130 wpm) leaves room for pauses. Read it aloud once with a timer and adjust. Bracketed notes like [ ] are places to drop in your own specific memory or detail — swap them for whatever's true rather than reading the brackets.*

---

## 1. Open with the honest struggle — 0:00–1:00
**[Slide 1: Title — "Why I Made Reforged"]**

Good morning. For those I haven't met, my name is Boaz, and I want to tell you about something I built — but really, I want to tell you about a problem I had, because the app only exists because of the problem.

Here's the honest version: for a long time, I wanted to be someone who was in the Word every day. I'd start strong — a new plan, a fresh journal, day one energy. And then life happened. A busy week. A tired morning. And the streak I never had would just... quietly end. I'd feel guilty, not open the app I *was* using, and a week would turn into a month.

If that sounds familiar to anyone in this room, that's exactly why I'm up here.

---

## 2. Name the deeper struggle — 1:00–2:15
**[Slide 2: "It wasn't a knowledge problem"]**

Here's what I eventually realized: it wasn't that I didn't *believe* Scripture mattered. I could've told you that "day and night" meditating on God's Word makes you like a tree planted by streams of water. I knew Psalm 1. I just wasn't living it.

It wasn't a knowledge problem. It was a *habit* problem. And I noticed that the tools I already trusted with my habits — the apps that got me to work out, to learn a language, to drink more water — all had something in common. They made the behavior small, they tracked it honestly, and they gently reminded you the next day. Meanwhile, my Bible reading had none of that. It was just me and my willpower, and my willpower kept losing.

I started asking a question that felt almost uncomfortable at first: what if the things that make secular habit apps effective aren't secular at all? What if consistency, gentle accountability, and remembering God's Word aren't in tension — what if I could build something that put them together, honestly, without turning Scripture into a gimmick?

---

## 3. The turning point — 2:15–3:15
**[Slide 3: phone screenshot — Home screen, "Good morning, Child of God"]**

I'm a [developer / engineer — adjust to your actual background], so I did what I know how to do: I started building. Not a big company, not a business plan — just something for me, to fix my own problem. A daily reading habit I could actually keep. A way to memorize Scripture that didn't rely on me white-knuckling it. Something that would meet me where I actually am, on my phone, in the cracks of a busy day.

That became Reforged.

---

## 4. What Reforged actually is — 3:15–5:00
**[Slide 4: three-screen collage — Bible reader / Scripture Memory / Home]**

So what is it, practically? Reforged is a Bible app built around one goal: helping God's Word actually stick — not just get read once and forgotten.

You can read Scripture in five different translations, right there on your phone, with the text laid out cleanly — no ads, no clutter. If you want to go deeper, you can tap any word and see what it means in the original Hebrew or Greek.

Then there's Scripture memory. This is the part I'm most excited about, because it's the part that solved my own struggle. Reforged uses something called spaced repetition — the same proven method language-learning apps use — to bring a verse back to you right before you'd naturally forget it. You practice it a handful of different ways: flashcards, fill-in-the-blank, typing it out. And slowly, without cramming, it moves from a verse you're reading to a verse you *know*.

And then there's the habit layer — the part that's admittedly a little playful. Reading streaks. A little encouragement when you show up again the next day. It's the same gentle nudging that gets people to close their fitness rings, except here it's pointed at something eternal instead of something that ends at the gym.

None of it is trying to replace your Bible, your church, or your small group. It's trying to be the thing in your pocket that makes the next twenty minutes with God's Word more likely to happen.

## 5. How it's changed my everyday life — 5:00–6:00
**[Slide 5: "What changed for me"]**

Here's what's actually different for me now, months in. I'm not reading because I'm disciplined — I still don't think I am. I'm reading because it's become the path of least resistance. It's the notification I already trust. It's the two minutes while coffee brews.

And the verses I've memorized aren't just sitting in a notes app somewhere — they come back to me. In traffic. In a hard conversation. At 2 a.m. with a worried mind. [Feel free to drop in a specific verse or moment here — a real one lands far better than a general one.] That's the whole point. Scripture that's only ever *read* can be forgotten by Tuesday. Scripture that's *hidden in your heart*, like Psalm 119 says, is there when you actually need it.

---

## 6. The bigger point — it's not really about the app — 6:00–7:00
**[Slide 6: "This isn't really about an app"]**

Now — I want to be careful here, because I don't want you to walk away thinking the takeaway is "download this app." That's not actually the point, and if you're happily in the Word every day already with a paper Bible and a highlighter, please, keep doing exactly that. Don't fix what isn't broken.

The point is underneath that: God's Word was never meant to be something we visit occasionally when we feel spiritual enough. It was meant to be daily bread. Ordinary. Built into the rhythm of a life, the same way we build in meals, or sleep, or checking in on the people we love.

Whatever gets you there — a reading plan on paper, a habit with a friend who texts you every morning, an app like this one, or just a decision you make today to open your Bible before you open your phone for anything else — the tool matters far less than the return.

---

## 7. Close — the challenge — 7:00–8:00
**[Slide 7: closing verse / call to habit]**

So here's what I'd love to leave you with. Not "go download Reforged" — though it's on the App Store, it's free, and if it helps you build this habit, I'd love for you to try it. What I'd love to leave you with is this: pick one small, honest way to be in God's Word tomorrow morning. Something so small you can't talk yourself out of it. One verse. One chapter. Five minutes before the day gets loud.

I built a tool because I needed help making that small thing repeatable. Maybe you need a different tool, or maybe you just need to hear someone else say out loud that they've struggled with this too, and it's not a sign that something's wrong with your faith — it's just being human.

"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path." That's not a lamp we're meant to glance at once a week. It's meant to go with us — every day, into every ordinary, unremarkable Tuesday. That's what I hope Reforged helps some of you do. And more than that, that's what I hope this church keeps helping each other do, together.

Thank you.
